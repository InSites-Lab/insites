### 5.1 Significance Statement

**(2-3 paragraphs, 200-280 words)**

**Output shaping (critical)**:
- Title: `## 5.1 Significance: [Site-Specific Theme]` — not a generic "Significance Statement." The title names what holds the principal significances together in this place.
- Opening sentence = the principal significances. Don't build up to them. State them, then support them.
- Each paragraph has ONE job: (1) the principal significances side by side, weaving all stages, (2) evidence basis and network connections, and any reading the assessment proposes, (3) what remains open or contested.
- This is the intellectual product of the assessment. Dense and precise — not ceremonial.

**What the statement is for.** A statement of significance says why this place matters, and to whom: what would be lost if the place were lost. It states the place's case plainly. The assessment's most original finding is a different thing.

**Principal significances.** Name every significance without which the place cannot be understood. There may be several. These tests help decide; none is required on its own:
- it says what the place was and did;
- the place still carries it, in its fabric, setting or use;
- people connected to the place hold it, as their words, practices or care for the place show;
- losing it would change what should be conserved.

Set the principal significances side by side and show how they relate, as layers of one place. Rank them only where the evidence supports a ranking, and do not merge them into a single claim.

**Readings the assessment proposes.** Where the assessment connects evidence into a new reading, give it as a further layer: say that it is proposed, state its basis, and mark it. However interesting, it adds to the principal significances and replaces none of them.

**Before drafting.** If Stages 1–4 leave it unclear which significances are principal, ask the expert.

**Opening Paragraph (mandatory)**:

Must explicitly weave together:
- Stage 1: Key contexts/timeline records
- Stage 2: Values and their meanings — now weighed through Stage 3–4 criteria
- Stage 3: Nara Grid findings (authenticity/integrity)
- Stage 4: Comparison with other assets

Show how these elements relate as layers of one place, each principal significance visible in its own right.
If Stage 3 rated documentary integrity as consequential, address it in the
significance statement — either as value (the record itself is heritage) or
as loss (uncompensated by documentation). Omit if unremarkable.

Where Stage 1–2 identified context-effects that extend beyond the asset — to connected sites, traditions, or regional themes — the significance statement must acknowledge the asset's role within that wider heritage network, not only its standalone value.

If Stage 1 or Stage 3 identified experiential or Spirit & Feeling content, weave it into the significance statement — not as a passing mention but as a thread. If no experiential evidence exists, note the gap.

**Evidence Mandate applies** — if a principal significance rests on 〰️ or 💭, state its basis within the sentence. Don't rely on notation alone.
